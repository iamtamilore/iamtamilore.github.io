The one thing I could not get anyone else to show me

blind, seeded, human-rated
80 generated garments, 4 conditions, rated by people who cannot see which condition produced what
Everyone building on image models shows you the good outputs. So I ran the opposite test. Eighty garment images across four generation conditions, presented to human raters in a seeded shuffle, with condition and filenames masked and a frozen rubric written before anyone rated anything. The rubric gates on one question first: is this image clear enough to judge at all. If the answer is no, the rest is skipped rather than guessed.
The interesting part is not the score. It is that an automatic OCR score and a human judgement disagree often enough to matter, and that the only way to find out which one is right is to ask a person who cannot see the pipeline.

How I work

  1. Establish the baseline before training anything. A model that cannot beat a simple guess does not deserve to exist. Pixel difference at 0.59 was the number that made 0.90 mean something.
  2. Name the error you can afford before choosing a threshold. Rejecting a good image costs one retry; accepting a bad one sends it to the client. That asymmetry is why the cutoff is 0.4821 and not 0.5.
  3. Model the process in BPMN before writing code. The automation should replace what actually happens, not what you think happens.
  4. Freeze the preprocessing with the model so training and serving cannot silently disagree about what a column means.
  5. Blind the evaluation. If the people rating the output can see which condition produced it, the measurement is decoration.
  6. State what breaks, in writing, next to what works. Every project page says what can go wrong before it says what works.
