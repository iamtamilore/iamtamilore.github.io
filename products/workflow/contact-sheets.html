Contact Sheets - how a beat becomes a still

Stage 2 of the 5-stage pipeline. The pipeline takes a beat, writes 5 queries, fetches candidate stills from the source article, ranks them by vs-hero similarity, and picks the highest-scoring candidate within the reject rules. Every step has a measured cost and a rule that closes one class of failures.

Stage 2: Select - from beat to contact sheet to chosen still

The selector reads the audio's beats.json and the brief's needs. For each need, it builds 5 queries, fetches candidate stills from the source article, computes ResNet50 cosine similarity against the hero reference frame, applies the reject rules, and selects the highest-scoring surviving candidate.

The five-query rule

Identical queries across needs put the same 50 jpgs in every folder. The selector dedups globally, so needs 7-16 silently get nothing. Five queries per need, character name first, plus a per-need verb.

queries = [
  "{character_name} {subject_action} {scene}",
  "{character_name} {scene}",
  "{character_name} {location}",
  "{subject_action} {scene} {visual_world}",
  "{location} {scene}",
]

This produces five meaningfully different still candidates per need rather than five variants of the same still. EP_GOJO rebuild cost ~$1.50 before this rule existed - four of 16 needs received the same still because three of their queries were identical.

The ranking - ResNet50 cosine similarity vs the hero reference

Every candidate gets scored against the locked hero reference frame using ResNet50 cosine similarity (vs_hero). Highest score wins, subject to the reject rules. The hero reference is held out of the active training run, so the calibration is not contaminated.

The reject rules - never generic, always named to the beat

A blanket rule like "no Kanao" or "no romance" rejected the wrong needs - the very need where the forbidden character IS the subject, or where romance IS the subject. Every NEVER names its beat. Hard character constraints stay absolute but the constraint is per-beat, not global.

accept: "{character} in {location}, looking {direction}, period dress"
reject: "modern product photography, neon lights, period-incorrect armour"
NEVER: "{character} appears with {forbidden_char}",
"modern-{period}-inappropriate costume",
"anachronistic weapons",

Two rejects of 23 shots were false positives at 640px thumbnail - at 1080x1920 they passed cleanly. Pixel review must be at full resolution. Open the source PNG, do not trust the 640px thumbnail verdict.

The retrieval index - what carries forward across episodes

Each source-article fetch produces a list of unique still URLs indexed against the hero reference. The index persists across episodes, so once a still has been seen and ranked for one character in one channel, the next episode reuses the embedding rather than re-embedding the same still. Index re-use is what makes the 4,527-chunk retrieval cheap on TermsGuard; the per-query work is just a cosine search against an index that already exists.

What a contact sheet actually looks like

This is the EP_MUSASHI contact sheet - 19 frames, each captioned with the line the narrator says during that shot and the timestamp window. Same data the build pipeline used to generate the MP4, rendered as a single page so a brand can verify each shot independently against the source.

The drift bug - what happens when the contact sheet is right but the audio is longer than the stills

EP_MUSASHI first build had the right 19 frames for the right 19 lines, but the stills were 5.16 seconds shorter than the audio. The builder rescaled every shot by 1.1479 to fit. The pictures walked ahead of the captions until they were 2.7 seconds out by the end of the video. 7 of 19 shots showed the wrong picture under the caption.

The fix: plan_shots.py gives each shot the pause in front of it, so the builder anchors every boundary to the words rather than to the spoken-span-only. scripts/common/repair_drift.py re-plans and rebuilds the episode. After the fix, the same 19 frames against the same audio produced a clean gate with zero defects.

The contact-sheet rules on this page are what prevent the drift from happening in the first place. The drift fix is the recovery path when it does.
